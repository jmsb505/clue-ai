# Market and reference review

**Status:** REFERENCE / EVIDENCE  
**Reviewed:** 2026-09-30  
**Method:** Public product pages and documentation, current official API and legal pages, and published job-seeker/employer research. Product claims are labeled as claims. Survey results are directional and limited to their stated samples and regions.

## Findings at a glance

- People benefit from accurate, current postings and useful information before they spend time on an application. Candidate research reports substantial concern about fake postings and low trust in automated hiring.
- JevJobs demonstrates a direct CV-to-ranked-results path. Its visible differentiators are weighted criteria, per-criterion score and confidence, posting links, and a clear notice that it neither applies nor verifies a job is still open.
- Jobbie's marketing is friendly and easy to scan. Its core interaction uses job cards with match percentages and swipe/skip actions, but the service is organized around automated applications. The card presentation can inform this product; its automatic submission is outside scope.
- Jev is a bounded decision model, not an explanation writer. Its score, probabilities, and confidence can support fit ranking, but they do not replace evidence, freshness checks, or an independent quality evaluation.
- Source access is fragmented. Public ATS APIs can provide employer listings, but they are company-specific. Broad aggregators may provide reach but have access limits, display/attribution obligations, cost, and retention conditions.
- The owner's budget allows at most $5/month for TypeSafe Jev and requires every other component and source to be free. This is a personal local app, not a commercial or public job board; source attribution and personal caching/use terms still need to be recorded.

## Reference product: JevJobs / Rezi

The live [JevJobs page](https://jevjobs.ai/) identifies the app as “JEV APPS” inside Rezi and presents the promise “Use Jev to discover jobs.” The page claims 4 million jobs and 10,000 new jobs added daily. The site says it searches jobs posted in the last 30 days, reads a resume to pick a role, location, criteria, and weights, and displays a match score for each criterion. It also says that the match is a guide, not an employment decision, that it does not apply, and that it does not check whether a posting is still open. Job-count, SOC 2, and data-sale statements are product claims, not independently audited findings in this review.

On 2026-09-30, the public sample-resume flow was exercised without uploading a personal CV. The visible sample used a PDF called `Jordan_Lee_Resume.pdf`; after search, the interface showed a role, location, and four weighted criteria. It returned three ranked examples, each linking to the source posting. The expanded first result displayed an aggregate match percentage and per-criterion score and confidence. The sample search reported 10.5 seconds. This is one observed demo run, not a performance guarantee or accuracy evaluation. The page asked the visitor to sign in to see all scored jobs and criterion details.

**Useful design patterns:** a clear first action, a short “what happens next” explanation, a resume review state, editable weights, a two-column profile/results workspace, strong and weak criteria, source links, and an explicit uncertainty/scope notice.

**Gaps to account for:** the first view does not disclose the full source catalog or scoring rubric; the site itself says it does not verify whether a posting remains open; and a sign-in gate hides the full list and all details. The local app should show source coverage, last-checked time, and the reason for a ranking without requiring sign-in.

Rezi's separate [job-search guide](https://www.rezi.ai/rezi-docs/job-search) describes a broader product with search, saved jobs, application status tracking, role/workplace/job-type/visa/skills filters, and company-page sourcing. Treat the 1.3-million-job figure on that guide as a Rezi claim; it differs from the current JevJobs page and is not a verified coverage count for JevJobs.

## Reference product: Jobbie

[Jobbie's public site](https://jobbie.bot/) leads with a bold promise, a prominent blue primary action, a phone-sized interactive job card, a short numbered workflow, feature sections, and an FAQ. Its demo card shows employer, ATS/source, title, location, posted time, match percentage, and skills, with skip/apply actions. Its navigation and app images suggest a saved jobs and applications workspace. The interface relies on short copy, visible progress, rounded cards, mobile patterns, and direct calls to action.

Jobbie's product page and FAQ describe resume parsing, role/location/pay preferences, job matching, resume tailoring, one-click applications, autopilot, and application tracking. The FAQ lists Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, Ashby, Paylocity, JazzHR, Workable, BambooHR, and Indeed as platforms it supports for applying. That list does not establish that these are the sources used to discover job listings. Marketing figures such as “100k+” users, applications per hour, and hours saved are not independently verified here.

Jobbie's [privacy policy](https://jobbie.bot/privacy) says profile details are sent to its AI provider when needed for generated resumes, cover letters, or application-screening answers; data is kept while the account is active; users can delete the account; and the company and service providers process data from the U.S. Its website describes automatic applications and the FAQ says Autopilot can submit without the user choosing each role. Those features and their associated data flow are outside this product's intended boundary.

**Design direction for this product:** keep the helpful tone, mobile-ready cards, visible match summary, fast scanning, and obvious source action. Replace Apply/Autopilot with **Save**, **Hide**, **Review match**, and **Open original posting**. Keep a details panel for why each criterion matched, where the evidence came from, and what is unknown. Use JevJobs' more explicit profile/criteria workspace as a complement to Jobbie's card presentation.

## What Jev can and cannot do

TypeSafe's [official introduction](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) describes Jev as an early-access “System One” model that returns structured decisions rather than free-form text. The [API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api) evaluates a state against typed questions. The [Score documentation](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/score) says a score rubric has ordered levels and returns a score, probability distribution, and confidence. The [confidence guide](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence) distinguishes a confidence summary from a probability distribution.

This maps well to a bounded fit evaluator: provide a candidate profile and one normalized posting, ask about role alignment and specific job-related criteria, and keep the model response structured. The model does not produce evidence-backed prose. Derive explanations from CV and job-description evidence in the application, and label the result as the user's fit estimate rather than a hiring outcome. Validate rubrics and score calibration on the platform's own data before relying on a composite score.

TypeSafe's current [homepage](https://typesafe.ai/) lists Jev at $42 per billion input tokens; its [launch post](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) says output tokens are free and acknowledges the company cannot establish that current pricing is not subsidized. At the listed input rate, $5 covers about 119 million input tokens before taxes and account-specific credit mechanics. The [Master Customer Agreement](https://typesafe.ai/legal/mca) bills through TypeSafe-managed credits and says automatic credit refills are optional; without opting in, TypeSafe may decline requests after credits are exhausted. The app applies a $4 rolling reserve to its own requests. On 2026-09-30 the owner waived further account-side refill, Order, and credit-conversion checks; those account facts remain unverified and the app makes no account-wide cap claim.

TypeSafe's [privacy policy](https://typesafe.ai/legal/privacy-policy) says it will not train or fine-tune models on API inputs, and that the services are hosted in the U.S. The current [Master Customer Agreement](https://typesafe.ai/legal/mca) allows API integration into a Customer Application for end users, while prohibiting the API being offered as a standalone service. It describes processing inputs to provide the service, permits telemetry derived from Customer Data to be processed without restriction, and says Customer Data may be deleted at TypeSafe's discretion. The agreement makes the customer responsible for user disclosures, notices, and rights; it also says Jev outputs can be inaccurate and must be independently evaluated. Its [Data Processing Addendum](https://typesafe.ai/legal/data-processing) provides transfer terms including EU Standard Contractual Clauses. These public documents do not establish account-specific retention, telemetry, or deletion behavior. The owner waived further TypeSafe-side verification for this personal local project; Clue keeps an in-app field disclosure, opt-in, and explicit score action. Public policy statements do not settle the user's choices about which profile fields to send.

## Job-search needs and market signals

Published survey results are not global market truth. The studies below are mostly U.S.-focused or use a limited set of countries; because users select their own geography, source selection and salary/work-eligibility fields need to adapt by country.

| Evidence | Finding | Product implication and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| [2026 Job Seeker Nation report page](https://www.jobvite.com/2026-job-seeker-nation-report/) | Jobvite reports that 1,500+ job seekers were surveyed; its page says 44% name not hearing back as their biggest hiring challenge, 53% have encountered a fake job post, and 34% think they were automatically rejected by AI. | Trust and listing authenticity deserve visible treatment. This is a company report summary, not a job-board-specific independent study; “not hearing back” happens after application and is outside this search product's control. |
| [2025 Employ Job Seeker Nation report](https://pages.lever.co/rs/659-JST-226/images/2025-Job-Seeker-Nation-Report.pdf) | A Zogby Analytics online survey of 1,500+ U.S. adults found 66% reported job-search burnout. The report says 31% used AI to support job search; among reported AI uses, 69% selected finding/matching relevant job listings. It also reports 37% had negotiated salary and 48% noticed increased salaries in postings similar to their current role. | Reduce noise and effort rather than maximize applications. Search relevance, salary visibility, and user control are reasonable priorities. The survey is U.S.-wide and includes workers and people seeking work. |
| [Greenhouse 2025 AI in Hiring report](https://www.greenhouse.com/uk/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair) | Greenhouse says its 4,136 respondents included 2,900 job seekers and 1,236 recruiters/managers in the U.S., U.K., Ireland, and Germany. Its U.S. subgroup found 46% of job seekers said trust in hiring fell, 42% blamed AI, and 8% said AI makes hiring more fair. | Make scoring understandable, keep the candidate in control, show uncertainty and evidence, and never imply the model predicts a hiring decision. This is a vendor survey and its AI questions concern hiring processes more broadly. |
| [NACE 2026 Job Outlook spring update](https://naceweb.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2026/publication/research-report/2026-job-outlook-report-spring-update.pdf) | In a new-graduate employer survey (163 responses for the resume-attribute chart), employers most often selected teamwork, computer skills, analytical/quantitative skills, creativity, and written communication among attributes sought on resumes. The report also says employers value examples of specific skills. | Fit should use the actual posting's requirements and evidence of what the candidate did, not just skill keywords. This U.S. survey is about new graduates; do not treat its ranking as a universal rubric. |

The most supportable product needs are: current and relevant roles, location/workplace filters, salary when available, a way to narrow by job type and work eligibility, source transparency, an understandable fit view, and ways to save or dismiss results. Rezi's own [search guide](https://www.rezi.ai/rezi-docs/job-search) lists workplace type, job type, visa sponsorship, skills, best-match/newest sorting, job details, saved jobs, and status tracking. That is useful product precedent, not independent evidence of market-wide preferences.

### Remote work from Italy and Europe

The first personal search profile is fully remote work from Milan/Italy. A “remote” label alone is insufficient: a role can be limited to U.S. residents, one country, EU/EEA, Europe, or worldwide. Ask separately where the user will work from and what work authorization they hold, preserve the employer's exact country/region language, and surface uncertainty when eligibility is unstated. The OECD's Europe study cautions that remote work is measured in more than one way across countries, which reinforces using employer eligibility evidence instead of treating all remote roles as cross-border roles ([OECD, *The New Geography of Remote Jobs: Evidence from Europe*](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/11/the-new-geography-of-remote-jobs-evidence-from-europe_08d989f6/29f94cd0-en.pdf)).

The initial source scan found promising free feeds for this target: Jobicy exposes a `geo` filter and `jobGeo` field and explicitly allows use in job-discovery apps with attribution; Remote OK and We Work Remotely invite feed use with attribution and links; Remote First Jobs offers public aggregator RSS; Startup Jobs offers a free API/RSS with country and remote filters. These are source candidates, not runtime-validated coverage. Jobicy's optional direct ATS link API costs $0.01 per job and is excluded. Himalayas has useful location restrictions but its API guidance conflicts with its general terms. See the [source discovery review](source-discovery-and-crawl-review.md) for each source's scope.

## Source coverage, access, and cost

No public API reviewed here gives a complete index of all job boards. Treat sources as connectors with explicit country coverage, personal-use terms, freshness, and costs. The owner has set a hard constraint: Jev may cost at most $5/month; every other recurring platform or data-source cost must be $0. The app runs locally, so there is no hosted infrastructure to scale in this phase. TypeSafe publishes a per-token list price, but it does not establish account-specific billing, taxes, or credit conversion.

For the Italy/Europe source assessment, including free personal-use APIs/feeds and their local-use conditions, see the separate [source discovery and crawl review](source-discovery-and-crawl-review.md).

| Source type | What current public documentation establishes | Fit for this product |
|---|---|---|
| Greenhouse employer boards | The [Job Board API](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html) returns published company jobs as JSON, and its GET endpoints are public without authentication. The endpoint is scoped to a company's board token. | Useful direct source with employer URL and structured fields. It does not provide a universal Greenhouse-wide role search or discover every company token. |
| Lever employer boards | The [Lever Postings API](https://github.com/lever/postings-api) lists published jobs under a company-specific site. Its documentation says it does not support full-text search over open jobs. | Useful direct source after finding a company's Lever board; coverage requires discovering and maintaining company site identifiers. |
| Adzuna | The [API overview](https://developer.adzuna.com/overview) describes job-ad search and requires an app ID/key. Its [terms](https://developer.adzuna.com/docs/terms_of_service) expressly allow personal research and set monthly request limits; displayed listings require “Jobs by Adzuna” credit and a link. | Trial candidate for the owner's local use. Confirm an Italian market endpoint and whether local caching/scoring falls within its personal-research allowance; exclude if a paid license is required. |
| Jooble | Its [REST API documentation](https://help.jooble.org/en/support/solutions/articles/60001448238-rest-api-documentation) describes keyword/location search, requires a country-specific key, returns a direct Jooble result link, and caps the free plan at 500 lifetime requests per key. | Bounded personal-use candidate after checking the Italian key and API terms. The quota is finite, so preserve calls for meaningful searches and cache only as the source terms allow. |
| Employer pages and structured data | Google's [JobPosting guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting) defines fields such as `datePosted`, `validThrough`, job location, remote eligibility, and salary, and says expired postings should not be included as live results. | Useful normalization schema and direct-site discovery signal. It is guidance for publishing eligible job pages, not a general job-search API. |
| LinkedIn and other closed boards | LinkedIn's [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) prohibits scraping/copying its services and unauthorized automation. Terms vary by platform. | Do not rely on scraping or logged-in browser automation. Use a documented source path or exclude the board. |

**Proposed source plan:** start with public APIs/feeds whose documentation supports personal use, then add official employer/ATS pages that can be read with ordinary low-rate requests. Confirm there is no fee, keep required credit and links, and use only the local caching the source allows. Do not use paid sources or infrastructure. Deduplicate locally, run Jev only on likely matches, and measure calls against the $5/month cap. Record `source`, canonical listing URL, source update time, first-seen time, last checked time, geographic scope, and any close/expiry signal. Show which sources were checked and when.

Before selecting providers, compare: accessible countries and language; unique active jobs; duplicate ratio; age/status accuracy; salary and remote eligibility coverage; redirect quality; personal-use terms; allowed local caching/retention; attribution; API limits; reliability; and whether recurring price remains $0 at expected search volume. The requested broad search is a coverage goal, not a promise that every board can technically or at zero cost be indexed. If the free source set is narrow, show that limitation instead of adding paid sources.

## Practical job-search process

The platform should support the candidate's search cycle without pretending a match score completes it:

1. Set one or more target role families, geography, work arrangement, compensation floor, and non-negotiables.
2. Review extracted CV facts and add relevant experience or skills that the document parser missed.
3. Search new postings and compare them against both the CV and user preferences. A CV omission is not evidence that the user lacks a skill.
4. Inspect match strengths, gaps, unknowns, job freshness, and original employer listing before acting.
5. Save promising roles, dismiss irrelevant ones, and tune preferences. If the user applies, they do it on the employer or authorized job-board site and may record the action in their profile.
6. Keep role-specific resume tailoring, networking, and interview preparation as separate future features; they are not a substitute for broad, accurate discovery.
